What are surplus funds?

At a foreclosure or tax sale, the home is sold to pay what was owed. If bidding goes higher than the debt, costs and other liens, there’s money left over. That leftover, called surplus funds, overages or excess proceeds, usually belongs to the former owner (or heirs).

The money often sits with a court, trustee or county because the notice went to an address nobody lives at anymore. Rules and deadlines vary by state and county.

Common questions

What are surplus funds?

When a home sells at a foreclosure or tax sale for more than what was owed, the extra money (the surplus, also called overage or excess proceeds) is usually owed to the former owner or other people with a legal interest.

Is this a scam?

It’s a fair question, and you should ask it of anyone. Real surplus funds are held by a court, trustee or county, and you can verify them there yourself. We never charge upfront and never ask for your SSN to check. See how to spot a scam.

Do I have to use a company?

No. In many states you can file a claim yourself. Some people prefer help because the paperwork, deadlines and competing claims can be complicated.
